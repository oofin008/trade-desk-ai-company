# Competitive positioning

Define how we win the message against specific competitors, for a specific buyer. This is a framework other skills draw from — not customer-facing copy.

## Read first
- `company/memory/COMPANY.md` — ICP, positioning, brand voice, product status (what's actually true)
- Competitor research (`departments/product/research/`); commission [[research-brief]] if it's stale or missing
- `company/decisions/LOG.md` — prior positioning decisions

## Procedure
1. **Name the competitors.** Real, specific alternatives the buyer actually considers — including "do nothing"/status quo and DIY.
2. **Map the buyer's criteria.** What this segment weighs when choosing (price, control, support, setup effort, trust).
3. **Build the comparison.** For each criterion: where we genuinely win, tie, or lose. Be honest about losses — they shape the angle.
4. **State the wedge.** The one or two dimensions where we win that the buyer cares most about → the core message.
5. **Attach proof.** Each differentiation claim needs evidence in COMPANY.md or research. Unprovable claim → cut it or flag for Product to make true.
6. **Draft messaging.** Positioning statement, 3 proof points, and objection handlers for our weak criteria.
7. **Save** to `departments/marketing/plans/[date]-positioning-[segment].md` and tag the founder.

## Output shape
```
**Segment / buyer:** [who]
**Alternatives considered:** [competitors + status quo + DIY]
**Buyer criteria (ranked):** [...]
**Comparison:** [criterion → win/tie/lose + evidence]
**Wedge:** [where we win × what they care about]
**Positioning statement:** [one sentence]
**Proof points:** [3, each cited]
**Objection handlers:** [our weak criteria → honest response]
```

## Guardrails
- No claim without evidence in COMPANY.md or research — especially "faster," "cheaper," "more secure."
- Don't disparage competitors with unverifiable claims; compare on facts.
- Positioning that implies an ICP or brand-strategy change is a founder/CEO decision — surface it.
- Feed gaps (claims we wish were true) to head-of-product, don't paper over them in copy.
